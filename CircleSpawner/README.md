# CircleSpawner

Builds shapes on screen out of borderless windows. The original app did one thing: enter a diameter and it filled a disk with blue windows, each a rectangle whose corners sit on the circle, so the overlapping rectangles together form the disk. That circle is still the first shape, and the same trick now draws a ring, a heart, a star, a word you type, the time or a picture you draw, in rainbow or sunset colors, growing in a few windows at a time. Pick another shape and the windows already on screen glide, stretch and change color into it instead of starting over; draw on the pad and the windows follow your pen, or draw a few frames and they play them as a flipbook. Press Spin and the whole shape turns round like a coin, in perspective. Click any window of the shape and it bursts apart; drag one and the whole shape comes along like a jelly, and can be thrown across the screen. Recovered from the git history of github.com/Nate314/site (`assets/javaApplications`).

## Controls / usage

- **Shape** (the row of seven buttons along the top): Circle (the original), Ring, Heart, Star, Word, Clock and Draw, each button showing a little picture of its shape in the chosen colors (the Word button spells `AB`, the Clock button `9:30`, and the Draw button shows the frame on the pad). The shape showing is in full color with a short bar under it, the others greyed; hovering a button names it. Pressing one turns the shape on screen into it (see Morphing), or builds it if nothing is showing.
- **Size**: about how many pixels across the shape is (default `200`). It must be a whole number from 2 to the room below the creator window (the smaller of the screen width and the height under the window); otherwise the window says which sizes work. A word is that tall, or smaller if it would not fit across the screen.
- **Colors** (the list under the shape buttons): Rainbow (the default), Blue (the original's color) or Sunset. The circle's slices are colored in the order they appear, since they share one center; the other shapes change color from left to right.
- **Word**: up to 12 letters, digits or `! ? . - ' :` (other characters are left out) in a 5 x 7 pixel font. Enter in this field builds the word.
- **Clock**: the time, spelled out in windows in the same font as a desk clock would show it (12-hour, `9:05:07`, local time) and kept going: every second the windows of the digits that changed glide into the next digit, in about 0.4 s, while the rest stay exactly where they are (a tick pairs windows by where they are on the screen, not by where they sit in their shape). Size, colors and scattering work as for a word. A scattered clock stops until you pick a shape or press Build it. If the screen is too slow to finish a change within the second, the clock skips that second rather than falling behind.
- **Draw**: shows a 20 x 20 drawing pad beside the controls (under them when the screen is narrower than 560 pixels, which makes the window taller). Press an empty cell and drag to draw, press a filled one and drag to erase. Every change reshapes the windows on screen within a few frames. **Mirror** (on at first) copies each stroke onto the other side of the blue middle line, so faces, hearts and butterflies come out even. **Clear** empties the pad, and the windows shrink away. The pad starts with a smiling face. A drawing is the Size across (made smaller, rather than refused, when the taller window leaves less room under it).
- **Flipbook** (the row under the pad): a drawing can have up to 12 frames, and the windows play them as a cartoon. **+** adds a copy of the frame shown just after it, to change; **<** and **>** show the frame before or after (the label says which, as `2 / 3`), and the windows on screen turn into it; **Delete** removes the frame shown (the last one stays). The frame before the one shown is drawn faintly under it on the pad (onion skin), so the next one can be drawn to match. **Play** turns the windows into the next frame about three times a second, round and round: each change glides in about 0.15 s, and the windows pair up by where they are on screen, as in a Clock tick, so the parts two frames share stay exactly still and only what changed moves. Drawing on the pad, **+**, **<**, **>**, **Delete**, Stop or clicking the shape stops it, and picking another shape stops it too. The first drawing has two frames, the smiling face and the same face winking, so Play has something to show at once. All the frames are saved.
- **Build it**: builds the chosen shape again from nothing, replacing the one on screen, growing in as at the start.
- **Spin**: turns the shape on screen round its upright middle line like a coin, one turn in about 2.4 s, until you press **Stop** (the same button). Each window is squeezed and moved to where its part of the flat shape would be seen from in front: the side swinging toward you is drawn a little taller and the far side shorter, the shape is brightest face on and dimmer edge-on, and past a quarter turn its back shows, mirrored (a word reads backwards) and darker. Stopping, or anything that changes the shape (picking a shape, colors, size or word, drawing, the flipbook's buttons or Play, Build it, dragging or clicking it), puts every window back flat in its place first. A Clock does not tick while it spins. With reduced motion on (in the browser) Spin says it is off and leaves the shape still. The spin is timed by the clock, so a slow screen shows fewer steps rather than turning slower, and the web page's Slow-mo slows it.
- **Send**: copies a link (on the desktop, a short code) that builds the chosen shape, in its colors, on a friend's screen: a word you typed, or your drawing with all its frames, so a flipbook sent to a friend plays as it opens. The link is the app's page with `?shape=` and a code: a letter for the shape, one for the colors, then the word (a space, `?` and `:` written as `_`, `~` and `*`) or each frame of the drawing as 67 characters, the frames joined by `.` (a 12-frame flipbook is about 800 characters). The home page and `play.html` pass it on too. On the desktop the code (or the whole link) is the first argument. A shape opened from a link is built at the visitor's own Size and is never saved: the visitor's own shape, word and drawing are still there next time.
- **On top** (the check box after Send): keeps every window of the shape in front of all other windows, on the screen (on the web page, in front of the other app windows), while it is checked, through morphs, spins and drags. Unchecked, only every 10th slice of the circle stays on top, as in the original. It is saved with the other choices.
- **Morphing**: changing the shape, the colors, the size (Enter in Size) or the word (Enter in Word) while a shape is on screen moves its windows into the new one over about two thirds of a second. Each new window comes from the old window at the most similar place in its own shape (the same corner, side or middle, and a similar size), nearest pairs first. When the new shape needs more windows, the extra ones split off old ones; when it needs fewer, the old ones left over shrink into the nearest new window and close. While the shape is flying apart, a change builds the new shape from scratch.
- The shape is centered under the creator window, which sits at the top center of the screen, and appears a few windows per frame, all of it in under a second. The line under the buttons says how many windows it took ("Heart built from 21 windows").
- **Clicking any window of the shape** scatters it: every window flies out from the middle, falls and closes.
- **Dragging any window of the shape** (pressing it and moving at least 5 pixels) picks the whole shape up. The windows under the pointer follow it exactly, and every other window springs after them, looser the farther it is, so the shape stretches out behind the pointer as it moves and wobbles back together when it stops. Let go to drop it there. Let go while moving and it is thrown: it glides on, slowing, bounces off the edges of the screen and comes to rest with every window back in its place. A shape dropped partly off the screen bounces back onto it. Grab it again while it is still gliding to catch it. With reduced motion on (in the browser) the shape moves as one piece and stops where it is let go. The shape then stays where it was put: changing the shape, colors, size or word, Clock ticks and the flipbook all happen there (moved back onto the screen if the new shape would not fit). **Build it** and scattering bring the next shape back under the creator window. Where it was put is not saved.
- The circle is built as before: window `i` (1 to the radius) is `2 * i` wide and `2 * sqrt(r^2 - i^2)` tall, its title is its index, and every 10th one is always on top. The other shapes are drawn on a grid (28 cells across, a word on its 7-pixel font) and covered with upright strips, each run of lit cells in a column joined to the same run in the column before it, so a heart takes about 20 windows rather than hundreds.
- The shape's windows have no title bar and never take the keyboard from the creator window. Closing the creator window exits the app and removes them all.
- The shape, size, colors, word, drawing, Mirror and On top are saved in `~/.circlespawner.txt` (`key=value` lines; each frame of the drawing is 20 rows of `#` and `.` joined by `/`, and the frames are joined by `|`; a save from before the flipbook reads as one frame), so the app opens on what you had last.
- One optional argument: a code (or link) from Send.

## Code layout

- `src/circlewindow/Main.java`: entry point; creates a `CircleCreatorWindow`.
- `src/circlewindow/CircleCreatorWindow.java`: the creator window; reads the controls, colors the pieces, builds a shape a few windows per frame and scatters it, each with a Swing timer.
- `src/circlewindow/Shapes.java`: turns a shape into the rectangles of its windows: the original circle slices, the ring, heart and star on a grid, and words; and scales a shape down to the picture on its button.
- `src/circlewindow/PixelFont.java`: the 5 x 7 pixel font for words and the clock.
- `src/circlewindow/CircleSliceWindow.java`: one undecorated window of solid color at a given place and size, which can be moved and recolored; pressing it scatters the shape.
- `src/circlewindow/Morph.java`: turns the windows of one shape into another: pairs old windows with new places, splits off or closes the difference, and moves them frame by frame.
- `src/circlewindow/Jelly.java`: the shape being carried and thrown: the springs that pull each window after the pointer, the glide and the bounces off the screen's edges.
- `src/circlewindow/Spin.java`: the shape turning like a coin: where each window goes and what shade it takes at an angle, and the angle after a time.
- `src/circlewindow/DrawPad.java`: the drawing pad, with Mirror, Clear, the flipbook's frames and onion skin, the first smiling (and winking) face and the saved form of a drawing.
- `src/circlewindow/Link.java`: Send's code: a shape, its colors and its word or drawing as characters a link keeps as they are, and back.
- `src/circlewindow/Saved.java`: reads and writes `~/.circlespawner.txt`.

## Self-check

`test/circlewindow/MorphCheck.java` checks that every shape button's picture fits its box, the morph pairing (the same places in two shapes pair up wherever the shapes are, every old window is reused when more are wanted, none is shared when fewer are), the drawn shape's size and place, an empty drawing, that a saved drawing reads back, the flipbook (the first drawing's two frames, that a frame turns into the next with its unchanged windows kept still, that every frame saves and reads back, that an older one-frame save reads as one frame, adding, deleting and the limit of 12 frames, and that playing wraps round), the clock's time text and colon, and that a clock tick keeps every window of the digits that did not change in place. From `CircleSpawner/`:

```
javac -d ../out/check/CircleSpawner src/circlewindow/*.java test/circlewindow/*.java
java -ea -Djava.awt.headless=true -cp ../out/check/CircleSpawner circlewindow.MorphCheck
java -ea -Djava.awt.headless=true -cp ../out/check/CircleSpawner circlewindow.JellyCheck
java -ea -Djava.awt.headless=true -cp ../out/check/CircleSpawner circlewindow.SpinCheck
java -ea -Djava.awt.headless=true -cp ../out/check/CircleSpawner circlewindow.LinkCheck
```

`test/circlewindow/JellyCheck.java` checks that the window under the pointer follows it exactly while a farther one lags, that a dropped shape comes to rest with every window exactly in place, that a throw carries it on and keeps it on the screen, that a shape dropped off the bottom comes back, and that with reduced motion the shape moves as one piece and stops where it is let go. `test/circlewindow/SpinCheck.java` checks that face on the shape is exactly as built, that from behind it is mirrored and darker, that edge-on a window is a thin line at the middle, that the side turned toward you looks bigger, and that a whole turn comes back to the start. `test/circlewindow/LinkCheck.java` checks that every shape and colors, a word with its odd characters and a three-frame drawing come back from Send's code as they were sent, that a code is read from a whole link, and that codes that are not shapes are refused.

## Running

From the repo root: `./run.ps1 old CircleSpawner` (or `./run.ps1 old CircleSpawner -Build` to build a jar).
