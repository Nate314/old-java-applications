# Clock

A desktop clock window with five tabs. Clock is the original app: an analog clock face (hour, minute and red second hand) or a digital readout of the day, date and time (now lit like a bedside LED clock), updated several times a second so the second hand sweeps smoothly, and now a flip clock whose cards fall over as the time changes. Timer is a kitchen timer on a 60-minute dial: drag the knob round and a red wedge shows the time set, then shrinks back to 12 as it counts down, and a bell rings when it runs out. Keep turning past 12 and it winds on in whole hours, up to four. Stopwatch times to the hundredth of a second, with laps. Focus runs work sessions the Pomodoro way: 25 minutes of focus, a 5-minute break that starts by itself, and a long break after every fourth session, with a bar for each of the last seven days showing the minutes focused. World shows where your people are in their day: a map of day and night with a dot for each city you pick (or the 24-hour dial with a hand for here and each city), and under it a strip of hours per city that rings in green the hours when everyone is at work, so you can find a time for a call.

The four dials (the clock face, the timer, the stopwatch and the focus ring) are drawn as objects sitting on the window rather than flat circles: a chrome rim lit from the top left with a shaded lip inside it, a face that shades from white to pale grey towards the bottom, a soft shadow cast down and to the right onto the window, the clock's and stopwatch's hands each casting their own shadow on the dial, and a faint glint of glass across the top.

The timer, stopwatch and focus sessions count against the wall clock, not the window's ticks, and are saved as they change. Close the window with a timer running and open it again later: it is still counting, or it has rung, and says when.

## Controls / usage

- **Tabs**: click Clock, Timer, Stopwatch, Focus or World at the top, or press **1** to **5** (or **C**, **T**, **S**, **F**, **W**). A running timer, stopwatch or focus session shows its time on its tab ("Timer 4:59", "Focus 18:22"), so it can be watched from the others. The line at the bottom lists the current tab's controls.
- **Clock**: left click (on mouse release) switches the face from analog to digital to flip clock and back to analog, right click between 12-hour and 24-hour time. The analog face is a proper dial: a tick for every minute, a longer one for every hour and the numbers 1 to 12 inside them, a broad hour hand and a long minute hand that reaches the minute ticks, both tapering to their tips, and a thin red second hand with a short tail and a red cap over the middle. While a focus session or break runs, the analog face shows it as a red (focus) or green (break) band round the rim, from the minute hand to when it ends.
- **Digital clock**: red seven-segment figures on a black panel, as on a bedside LED clock, with the unlit segments faintly showing behind them. The hours and minutes are big, with a colon that blinks each second (steady when the browser page asks for reduced motion), and the seconds small beside them, under AM and PM lights in 12-hour time (the hour has no leading zero). Under them a row of the weekdays with today lit, the date in small figures (month, day, year) and, while the alarm is on, when it rings.
- **Flip clock**: the hours and minutes on four big split-flap cards and the seconds on two small ones, dark grey with white figures, as on a bedside flip clock. When a figure changes, the top half of its old card falls forward over the hinge, faster as it goes, and lands as the new card's bottom half (the big cards in about half a second, the seconds a little faster); with the system asking for reduced motion in the browser the cards change without falling. In 12-hour time the hour has no leading zero (its first card is blank) and AM or PM shows over the seconds. Under the cards are the day and date and, while the alarm is on, when it rings. Switching between 12 and 24 hours flips the hour cards too. The cards are drawn once per size into half-card images, since an image's edge is the only clipping the browser build has.
- **Alarm**: the analog face has a fourth hand, as a wind-up alarm clock does: a thin brass hand with a round knob, grey while the alarm is off. Drag the knob round the face to set the alarm in five-minute steps; like a real alarm clock's hand it means the next time the hour hand gets there, so at 6 am the hand on 7 is 7 am and the hand on 5 is 5 pm. Setting it turns the alarm on. **Up** / **Down** move it five minutes either way (on the digital face too, where the button shows the time). The **Alarm off** / **Alarm 7:00 am** button in the top right corner (or **A**) turns it on and off, and under the middle of the face it says how long until it rings ("Rings in 8 h 5 min"), or over the middle while the knob is in the lower half, so the knob never covers it. It rings every day at that time: the window switches to the Clock tab, the clock shakes (not in the browser when the system asks for reduced motion), the title says "Wake up!" and a bright rising phrase rings on the bell for about a minute. **Space**, **Enter** or a click on the face snoozes it for 9 minutes (the title counts the snooze down, "Snoozing 8:59"); **Stop** (the corner button, **A** or **Esc**) silences it until tomorrow. Like the chimes, it rings only while the window is open, and one found more than ten minutes late (the computer slept) is skipped rather than rung. It starts off, set to 7:00 am.
- **Chimes**: the **Chimes off** / **Chimes on** button in the Clock tab's top left corner (or **B** on the Clock tab) turns on the Westminster quarters, the tune of Big Ben's clock tower, on the bell of the MIDI synthesizer: one four-note change at quarter past, two at half past, three at quarter to, and four on the hour followed by one stroke of a low bell per hour (twelve at noon and midnight). They ring on whatever tab is showing. Turning them on rings the quarter just gone at once, so you hear them (in the browser that click also lets the page play sound); turning them off silences them. Each bell struck sends a brass ring in from the rim of the analog face, further and bolder for the hour bell. Only a quarter that begins while the window is open rings, not one missed while it was closed or the computer slept, and the timer's and the focus sessions' bells cut the chimes short. While the alarm is on, they keep quiet for the eight hours before it rings, so they do not strike through the night. They start off.
- **Timer**:
  - Drag the knob (or click anywhere on the dial) to set whole minutes. Turning on past 12 winds into the next hour, up to four hours, and turning back past 12 unwinds it; the knob stops at 0 and at four hours. While whole hours wait after the one on the wedge, the rest of the dial is shaded pink and a pill under the middle says how many ("+1 h"), so 1 h 25 min shows as a 25-minute wedge and +1 h: the wedge shrinks back to 12, then shows the next hour full. A click on the dial picks the minute pointed at in the hour already set. Turning it while the timer runs keeps it running from the new time. Over an hour the time reads as hours, minutes and seconds ("1:25:00").
  - The preset buttons set 3, 5, 10 or 25 minutes, 1 hour or 2 hours.
  - **Start**, **Pause** and **Resume** (or **Space** or **Enter**); **Reset** (**R**) goes back to the full time. **Up** / **Down** add or take away a minute.
  - Under the time left it says when the timer will end ("Ends at 3:42 pm"). The window title says the time left too ("4:59 left - Clock"), so it shows on the taskbar.
  - When it runs out the window switches to the Timer tab, flashes pink and rings a tubular bell phrase on the MIDI synthesizer every few seconds, eight times, and says "Rang at 3:42 pm". **Stop** (or a click anywhere else on the tab, or any key but R and the tab keys) silences it and leaves the timer ready at the same time; **Again** (**R**) starts the same time over.
- **Stopwatch**: **Start** / **Stop** (**Space** or **Enter**), **Lap** while running (**L**), **Reset** once stopped (**R**). The dial is a stopwatch's: the seconds 5 to 60 round it, a red hand right across it that goes round once a minute, a small dial above the middle counting the minutes (once round in 30) and the time to the hundredth under it, in one size so it does not jump as the figures change. Once a lap is done, each new lap races the best one: a band runs round the rim from where the lap began, green for as long as the best lap took and red for the time lost after that, a green mark stands where the best lap would have ended, and under the time it says how far ahead or behind it is ("2.26 s behind best"). A band longer than a minute stops at one turn. Laps are listed newest first with each lap's time and the total, the fastest in green and the slowest in red, each with a bar as long as the lap (the slowest fills the column) so they compare at a glance (up to 99 laps).
- **Focus**:
  - A ring drains from 12 o'clock as the session runs, red while focusing and green on a break. Inside it: which session of four ("Focus 2 of 4") or which break, the time left, when it ends, and a dot per session of the set, filled once done.
  - **Start**, **Pause** and **Resume** (**Space** or **Enter**). When a session ends, a falling bell phrase rings twice, the window switches to the Focus tab and the break starts by itself; when the break ends a rising phrase rings and the next session waits for Start. After four sessions the break is a long one (three short breaks long), and then a new set begins.
  - **Skip** (**N**) ends the session or break early. The minutes already focused count towards the day, but a skipped session does not count towards the set.
  - **R** goes back to the first session of a set. The lengths button (**L**) cycles 25 / 5, 50 / 10 and 15 / 3 minutes (focus / short break); changing it starts the current session or break over at its new length.
  - Under the ring, today's minutes and sessions, the total for the last seven days, and a bar per day (today in red, on the right). The window title shows a running session too ("Focus 18:22 - Clock", or "Break 3:10 - Clock" on a break).
  - A session or break that ended while the window was closed is logged at its own time; the bell rings only if it ended less than a minute ago.
- **World**:
  - The map (shown first) is the whole world laid flat, lit where the sun is up at the time shown and dark where it is night, with a soft dusk between (the sun 0 to 12 degrees below the horizon). The sun stands as a yellow disc where it is overhead, and each picked city is a dot in its hand's color, named beside it. Dragging the strips or stepping Earlier and Later moves the night across the map, so a call planned for 8 am here shows whether it is dark in Sydney. The sun's place is worked out from the date with the usual low-precision formulas (the self-check holds the solstices and equinoxes to 0.05 degrees); the coastlines are Natural Earth's (public domain) on a one-degree grid. Here has no place on the map, since the computer's clock gives only its time zone.
  - **Dial** (**M**, or a click on the map) swaps the map for the 24-hour dial, and **Map** (**M**, or a click on the dial) brings it back; the choice is kept.
  - The dial is a day of 24 hours with noon at the top and midnight at the bottom, its ring colored by the hour: dark blue for the night (10 pm to 7 am), light blue for the morning and evening, and yellow for working hours (9 am to 5 pm). Here has the thick red hand and each picked city a hand of its own color, named on the face. Beside the dial: the time and date here, and how far the time shown is from now.
  - Under it, a row for here and each city: its name, how far ahead or behind here it is (and "tomorrow" or "yesterday" when its date differs), its time, and a strip of here's day from midnight to midnight, each hour cell showing that city's hour in the same colors. A white tick marks where the city's day changes. The hours when everyone is at work are ringed in green across the strips, and the line under them says when ("Everyone at work: 9:00 am to 11:00 am here"); when no working hour suits everyone it gives the hours everyone is awake, or failing that the hour with the most people awake.
  - Drag along the strips to show any time of the day (in quarter hours): the red line, the dial's hands and every row follow. **Earlier** and **Later** (**Left** / **Right**) step a whole hour; **Now** (**Space**, **Enter**, **Home** or **Esc**) goes back to the time now.
  - **Cities** (**A**) lists 54 cities with their time now; click one to add or remove it, up to five. **Done** (**Enter**, **Esc** or **A**) goes back. London, New York and Tokyo are picked to start with.
  - Daylight saving is worked out from each city's rules (United States and Canada, Europe, south-east Australia, New Zealand, or none) in `Zone.java`, so the times agree on the desktop and in the browser. Here's own offset comes from the computer's clock.
- No command-line arguments.
- The window is a square one quarter of the screen width wide, but at least 380 pixels (and never bigger than the screen). The current time text is also printed to stdout each time it changes (once a second).

## In the browser

On the web site the clock follows you: while a timer, stopwatch or focus session runs, every other page shows it in its toolbar, rings when it ends, and offers a quick game for a break, then calls you back to focus (see [Clock follows you](../web/README.md#clock-follows-you)).

## Saved state

`~/.clock.txt` (in `user.home`) keeps the tab, the face (`face=analog`, `digital` or `flip`), 12 or 24 hours, the timer (its length, and when it ends while running or the time left while paused) the stopwatch (when it started while running, the time banked before the last stop, and the laps), the focus session (the lengths, session or break, the sessions done in the set, and when it ends while running or the time left otherwise) the minutes focused and sessions finished on each of the last 14 days, the World tab's cities (`world=London,New York,Tokyo`) and whether it shows the map or the dial (`worldMap=on` or `off`), whether the chimes are on (`chimes=on`), and the alarm: its time in minutes after midnight (`alarm=420` for 7:00 am), whether it is on (`alarmOn=on`), and when a snooze rings again (`alarmSnooze=`, a wall clock time, only while one waits). A missing or damaged file, or a damaged line, leaves the defaults: the Clock tab and a 5-minute timer.

## Code layout

- `src/clock/Main.java`: the `JFrame`; holds the current date/time fields, loads the state, starts a 50 ms Swing `Timer`, keeps the title up to date and rings when the timer runs out.
- `src/clock/ClockTicker.java`: the timer's `ActionListener`; reads the local time from a `Date`, formats the time text, prints it, checks the countdown and repaints.
- `src/clock/ClockPanel.java`: draws the tab strip and the help line, and routes the mouse and keys to the current tab.
- `src/clock/Tab.java`: what a tab does (label, help line, paint, mouse and keys).
- `src/clock/ClockFace.java`: the original analog and digital clock, and the flip clock's place among them.
- `src/clock/DigitalFace.java`: the digital clock: the seven segments of each figure, the blinking colon, the AM and PM lights and the weekday row.
- `src/clock/FlipFace.java`: the flip clock: its six figures, the half-card images and the falling flaps.
- `src/clock/TimerView.java` and `src/clock/Countdown.java`: the timer's dial and controls, and the countdown itself.
- `src/clock/StopwatchView.java` and `src/clock/Stopwatch.java`: the stopwatch's ring, laps and controls, and the stopwatch itself.
- `src/clock/FocusView.java` and `src/clock/Focus.java`: the focus ring, the week of bars and the controls, and the sessions, breaks and day log themselves.
- `src/clock/WorldView.java` and `src/clock/Zone.java`: the world dial, the strips, the city picker and the hours that suit everyone, and the cities' time zones, their daylight saving rules and the calendar math behind them.
- `src/clock/WorldMap.java`: the World tab's map: the land as runs of sea and land per degree of latitude, where the sun is overhead, how high it stands at a place, and the picture of day and night (made again once per minute of the time shown). The cities' latitudes and longitudes are in `Zone.java`.
- `src/clock/Alarm.java`: the alarm's time, when it next rings (worked out with `Date`'s local-time constructor, so daylight saving changes are kept), the snooze, and the night before it when the chimes keep quiet.
- `src/clock/Chime.java`: the bell, played with Swing timers so the event thread never sleeps (a system beep where there is no synthesizer).
- `src/clock/Westminster.java`: the notes of each quarter's chime and the hour's strokes.
- `src/clock/ClockState.java`: reads and writes `~/.clock.txt`.
- `src/clock/Ui.java`: the shared colors (the original cyan, black, white and red), fonts, buttons and time formats.

The clock used to read the time from `Calendar`. It now reads `Date`'s local-time getters, which give the same time on the desktop and give local time in the browser, where TeaVM's `Calendar` is UTC.

## Running

From the repo root: `./run.ps1 old Clock` (or `./run.ps1 old Clock -Build` to build a jar).

## Self-check

`test/clock/TimerCheck.java` checks the countdown (start, pause, resume, running out exactly once, the four-hour cap, Up and Down while running, turning the knob on past 12 into the next hour and back, stopping at 0 and four hours, a click picking the minute in the hour set, times over an hour as hours, minutes and seconds), the stopwatch's laps, fastest and slowest, the lap under way against the best lap (ahead, then behind, a single lap counting as the best) and the dial's band (green while ahead, red for the time lost, the green mark at the best lap's end), focus sessions (breaks starting by themselves, catching up after the window was closed, the long break after four, skips, lengths, the day log), the time formats, and that the state file round-trips a running timer, stopwatch and focus session, rings a timer that ran out while closed at its own time, and survives damaged lines, the notes and strokes of the Westminster quarters, the digital clock's segments (each figure lights its own set, the count a seven-segment display uses), the flip clock (its figures in 12 and 24 hours, a flap falling from standing to lying down, the face remembered), and the analog dial (an hour tick at 12, bare face between minute ticks, the numbers, the middle and the alarm knob's circle at every hour kept clear), the dials' look (the rim lit at the top left and dark at the bottom right, the shadow cast below the dial and not above), and the alarm (ringing once on time, the snooze, stopping until tomorrow, skipping one found late or missed while closed, the hand's 12-hour dial meaning the next time that hour comes round, the quiet night before it, and the saved lines). `test/clock/WorldCheck.java` compares every city's offset with the JDK's time zone database every six hours from 2023 to 2030 and every quarter hour around each daylight saving change (about 640,000 instants), checks the calendar math day by day over 400 years, the hours that suit everyone, and that the saved cities round-trip, with unknown names, repeats and more than five dropped. It also checks the map: land and sea the right way up, every city on or one degree from its coast and roughly where its time zone says, the sun over the tropics at the 2024 solstices and the equator at the March equinox, its longitude at noon UTC (the equation of time), London's midwinter noon sun at 15 degrees and its midnight dark while Tokyo has its morning, and that the map or dial choice is saved. From `Clock/`:

```
javac -d ../out/check/Clock src/clock/*.java test/clock/*.java
java -ea -cp ../out/check/Clock clock.TimerCheck
java -ea -cp ../out/check/Clock clock.WorldCheck
```
