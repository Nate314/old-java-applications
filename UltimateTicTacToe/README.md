# UltimateTicTacToe

An UltimateTicTacToe game written in Java, against the computer, for two players on one screen, or with a friend anywhere by sending links (or codes) back and forth, one move each. Nine small tic-tac-toe boards sit in a 3x3 grid; the cell you play in decides which board your opponent must play in next, and pointing at a cell shows which one before you commit.

## Controls / usage

- **Mode buttons** (top of the window): Computer: Easy, Computer: Hard, Two players and Friend by link (Easy, Hard, 2 players and By link when the full labels are wider than the board). The current mode is lit; picking one starts a new game in it, except Friend by link, which goes back to the game with your friend if one is on. Against the computer you are X and move first; the computer answers as O after a short pause, and clicks wait until it has moved.
  - Easy wins the game or a small board when it can, and otherwise plays a random legal move.
  - Hard runs a Monte Carlo tree search: for about 0.7 s (and at least 300 games) it plays random games from the position, steers the search toward the moves that keep winning, and plays the move it explored most.
- The board is painted on a dark slate: each cell is a rounded tile, X is drawn in blue and O in coral, and a mark draws itself in when it is played (X stroke by stroke, O round from the top). The last move sits on a lighter tile.
- **Click a cell** to place a mark. X moves first, then players alternate.
- The first move can be on any board. After that you must play on the board matching the cell your opponent just picked. The boards you may play on are lighter and outlined in the color of the side to move.
- **Point before you play**: with the mouse over an empty cell you may play, a faint mark shows there, the board (or boards) that move would send your opponent to gets an outline in their color, and the status line says it in words ("Sends the computer to the top right board.", "Wins the board! They go to the top right.", "This move wins the game!").
- A small board that is won gets a white line struck through its three marks, then the winner's big X or O drawn over the whole board, on a tint of their color, with the small marks faded under it; a drawn (full) board turns gray. Finished boards are closed. If you are sent to a finished board, you may play on any open board.
- When the game is won, the three winning boards are ringed in gold and the rest fade back.
- Win three small boards in a row to win the game. The title bar and the status line under the board show whose turn it is and the result; click any cell after the game ends to start a new one.
- Against the computer, a second status line shows your record on that level (wins, losses and draws). The record, the mode played last, the game with a friend (its code, and whether it waits for the friend) and whether Sound is off are stored in `.ultimatetictactoe-record.txt` in your home folder, so the next start opens in that mode.
- **Friend by link**: play someone who is not at your screen, one move per message. You are X and move first; your move puts the game on the clipboard (Copied! in the status line) and the board waits. Send it to your friend however you like. They open it, play O, and their move is copied for them to send back; open that one and it is your move again. The friend's last move is on the lighter tile.
  - On the web the game is a link to the game's page, such as `UltimateTicTacToe/index.html?game=e5e1`; opening it starts the page on that position, with a line on its title screen saying a friend sent a game. On the desktop it is the code alone (`e5e1`).
  - The row under the board shows the link (or code) to send, with **Copy link** (Copy code) to copy it again and **Take back** to undo your move before you send it. To open a friend's link or code, paste it into the field and press **Open** or Enter. A link or a code works in either version.
  - A code is the game's moves, two characters each: the board as a letter a-i and the cell as a digit 1-9, both in reading order, so `e5` is the very middle. Opening one replays every move by the rules, so a code with an illegal move does not open ("That is not a game link").
  - The game is kept in the record file (below), so closing the window does not lose it, and switching to another mode only sets it aside. Opening the same link again after you answered it (reloading the page) keeps your answer.
- **Review game** (or **R**) once a game has ended, in any mode: look the game over move by move, with the computer's view of every move. The button sits at the right of the record line ("Hard: 3 wins, ..."), and in Friend by link it takes the place of Open. The mode buttons give way to a chart of the game and the record line to a row of **<**, **>**, **Next mistake** and **Done**; the board opens on the final position.
  - The computer runs its Hard search on every position of the game, one at a time (about 0.12 s each, so a game of 40 moves takes several seconds, and the browser page waits that long each time), and the chart fills in as it goes: one column per position, rising in blue while X's chances are above even and falling in coral while O's are. X's chances are the share of the search's random games from that position that X won, a draw counting half; the chart stretches small leads so the opening still shows. Click or drag on the chart to jump to a position; the one on the board is outlined in white.
  - Each move gets a grade. When it was the search's own pick it is a **Best move** (a green "!" badge on its tile). Otherwise the position the pick would have led to is searched too, and the move is graded by how much lower its maker's chances were after it than after the pick: up to 6 points is **Good** (no badge), up to 12 an **Inaccuracy** (yellow "?!"), up to 25 a **Mistake** (orange "?") and more a **Blunder** (red "??"). Inaccuracies and worse get a dot of their color on the chart, and on the board the search's pick is shown as a faint mark in a gold ring. The status line names the move and its grade, with its maker's chances after it and after the pick ("Move 26, O: Mistake (27%, best 42%)"); against the computer the sides are "you" and "the computer".
  - The search is the random-game search Hard plays with, run briefly, so its numbers are estimates that move a little from one review to the next; a move well inside a grade keeps it, one near the edge may not.
  - **<** and **>** (or the left and right arrow keys; up and down too) step back and forward, a step forward drawing the move in; Home and End go to the start and the end. **Next mistake** (N) jumps to the next mistake or blunder of either side. At the end, the status line gives the result and the turning point (the move that cost the most), and the title bar counts each side's inaccuracies, mistakes and blunders. **Done** (Esc, or R again) goes back to the finished game; a click on a cell does nothing while reviewing. Nothing is saved.
- **How to play** (or **L**): six short steps on the real board, one rule each, for anyone new to the game. The button sits at the right of the record line before the first move of a game while hints are off (not in Friend by link, where L still works). Each step sets up a position and gives X one task in the status line, with the rule under it: send O to the middle board, play on the board you were sent to, win a small board, play anywhere after being sent to a finished board, do not send O to a board it can win, and win the game. A move that does the task is played and explained; any other is turned away with the reason ("That sends O to win the top board.") and the board stays as it was. The mode buttons give way to **Back**, **Again**, **Next** (lit once the step is done; a click on the board also goes on) and **Done** (Esc, or L again), which brings back the game as it was. After the last step, **Play** starts a game against the computer (on Easy, unless a computer level was already on). Nothing is saved or counted.
- **Hints** (or **H**): on your turn, every cell you may play shows your chances if you play there (in Two players, the chances of the side to move), from the same random-game search Hard plays with (the share of its games after that move that you won, a draw counting half). The search runs in short slices, about 0.9 s in all, so the numbers settle on the board as it thinks. Its pick gets a gold ring, and each tile leans to the color the review would grade that move against the pick, counting only the part of its shortfall bigger than the search's doubt about it (two standard errors, from how many games it got): green for the pick, a little lighter for Good, then yellow, orange and red for an inaccuracy, a mistake and a blunder. The status line names the pick's chances once the search is done, and a screen reader hears each cell's chances with its name ("empty, open, 57%, best, middle board, middle square"). The button sits at the right of the record line from the first move until the game ends, and before the first move too while hints are on (How to play has that place while they are off; not in Friend by link, where H still works), and hints stay on, game after game, until Hints or H again. Like the review's, the numbers are estimates; each starts from 50% as if ten games had been drawn, so a move the search tried only a few times stays near even instead of showing as a blunder (or a sure win) by chance. The browser searches fewer games in the same time, so its numbers are rougher. A game against the computer in which hints were shown does not count in the record ("With hints, this game won't count.").
- **Sound** (the last of the mode buttons, lit while on, shown as a note when the labels are shortened and left out on a board too small for even that; or **M**, which always works): the game plays a tune as it goes. Every cell of a small board has its own note of a C major pentatonic scale, rising in reading order (top left lowest, bottom right highest), so where a mark lands can be heard: X plays it on the piano, O on the guitar an octave lower. A board won plays the three notes of its winning line in turn as the line strikes through it, and the game's win plays the three winning boards' notes (each board's place on the big grid giving its note) an octave up, as the gold rings in; a drawn board or game is two soft falling notes. A click where the next move may not go answers with a short, soft low note, and so does a move How to play turns away. Stepping forward through a review (>) plays each move's note again, so a game can be listened back. Sound is on to begin with; off is kept in the record file (`sound=off`). On the desktop the notes use the General MIDI piano and nylon guitar; in the browser the shim's synthesizer plays each as a plain tone of the same pitch and length, and the site keeps the home page's demo (a page opened with `?watch`) quiet until the visitor takes over.
- **W**: watch the computer play both sides, each move a short (150 ms) Hard search, game after game, the next starting 3 s after each ends. From Two players or Friend by link it first starts a game against the computer on Easy (the game with a friend is only set aside). W again, a click on a cell or a click on a mode button takes over: X is yours again, in the game as it stands. A game the computer played X in does not count in the record.
- **Keyboard**: Tab and Shift+Tab move between the cells and buttons (Swing's focus order; in the browser too), and Space or Enter presses the focused one. The arrow keys move a white cursor across the whole 9x9 grid (from board to board at the edges) and preview the cell they land on, as the mouse does. A screen reader hears each cell's mark, whether the next move may go there and where it is ("empty, open, top left board, middle square"; "O, last move, top board, bottom left square"), through Swing's accessible names. One optional command-line argument: a friend's code or link, which opens it in Friend by link (`./run.ps1 old UltimateTicTacToe e5e1`). The browser build also passes the page's own address, for the links. The window is about 3/4 of the screen height (narrower on a narrow screen, so it fits) and not resizable; the marks scale with the cells.

## Code layout

- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/Main.java`: entry point; creates the `GameFrame`.
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/GameFrame.java`: the window; holds the mode buttons, the nine boards, the status lines and the game state, times the drawing in of marks and wins (repainting the cells on a Swing timer while they draw), works out what the cell under the mouse or the cursor would do, and asks the computer for its moves on a Swing timer (after the player's move has drawn in), for both sides while watching (W); also runs the review (R): the chart and the review row in place of the mode buttons and the record line, stepping through positions, and one position's search per Swing timer tick.
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/GameState.java`: the rules: marks, small board results, the active board, whose turn it is, the last move and the overall winner; also copies a game and lists its legal moves.
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/Mode.java`: the four modes.
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/FriendLink.java`: the game codes of Friend by link: moves to and from codes, codes in pasted links, and the links to send.
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/Computer.java`: the computer's moves: Easy, and Hard's Monte Carlo tree search, which also gives the review its picks and chances (`analyse`) and the hints theirs (`Think`, a search grown a slice at a time).
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/Review.java`: a finished game under review: its positions, each one's chances and the search's pick, searched one step at a time, and the grade of each move.
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/ReviewGraph.java`: the review's chart of X's chances, with the grade dots, the position shown, and click or drag to jump.
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/Lesson.java`: How to play: each step's position, rule, task and the check that a move does the task.
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/Sounds.java`: the game's notes on the MIDI synthesizer: each cell's note, a won line's three, a draw's and a refused click's; `GameFrame` plays them as each move draws in, in time with the drawing. A silent copy lists what it was asked to play, for the self-check.
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/Record.java`: reads and writes the record against the computer, the last mode and the game with a friend.
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/SmallBoard.java`: one 3x3 board; lays out its cells.
- `src/ultimateticktacktoe/CellButton.java`: one cell; paints its tile and mark, plus its slice of anything drawn across its board or the grid (outlines, the line through a won board, the big mark, the gold of a winning board: each cell draws the whole shape in its board's coordinates and keeps the part that falls on it), forwards clicks and arrow keys to the game, and names itself for screen readers.

## Running

From the repo root: `./run.ps1 old UltimateTicTacToe` (or `./run.ps1 old UltimateTicTacToe -Build` to build a jar).

## Self-check

`test/ultimateticktacktoe/GameStateCheck.java` checks small board wins and draws (and the line a win is struck through), the send-to-board rule and the overall winner, copies and legal moves, that both computer levels take a winning move, that over ten games every computer move is legal and Hard (with a short search) beats Easy at least seven times, and that a whole game's code replays to the same position, malformed or illegal codes do not open, and codes are read from links and pasted text. It also reviews a whole game (every position gets chances, exact at the end, every move a grade and a legal better move, each counted once), checks the grade thresholds, and checks that a move missing a game-winning move is not graded best while the win itself is, with the win as the better move. The hints' search, grown in slices, has chances (between 0 and 1) for every legal move and no other, and picks a game-winning move with near-certain chances; its doubts shrink as it grows. And every How to play step starts as a live game, X to move, that some legal move completes and (except the step where every move on the board you were sent to is right) some legal move misses. For Sound: each cell's note rises in reading order, O's an octave under X's, a won line plays its three notes in order, off is silent, and `sound=off` is kept in the record file. From `UltimateTicTacToe/`:

```
javac -d ../out/check/UltimateTicTacToe src/ultimateticktacktoe/*.java test/ultimateticktacktoe/*.java
java -ea -cp ../out/check/UltimateTicTacToe ultimateticktacktoe.GameStateCheck
```
